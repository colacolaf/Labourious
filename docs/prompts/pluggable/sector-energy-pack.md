# Sector Pack — Integrated Oil + E&P + Oilfield Services

## Bottom line framing

Energy is a **commodity-pulse + capex-discipline** business. Five
sub-segments with different vectors:

1. **Integrated Oil (XOM, CVX)** — driven by upstream price realisations
   *plus* downstream crack spreads. Their thesis is diversification:
   upstream prices fall, downstream margins widen. The reverse is also
   true. A pure upstream read misses half the P&L.
2. **Pure E&P (EOG, OXY, VLO, MPC)** — single-axis: commodity realisations
   minus hedge ratio. Capex discipline is the second axis (return-on-
   capital has to clear hurdle rate or the thesis fails on capital
   allocation grounds alone).
3. **Refining (VLO, MPC)** — crack spread + turn-around calendar.
   Quarterly refiner margins swing 30%+ around maintenance schedule.
4. **Oilfield Services (SLB, HAL, BKR)** — global rig count (Baker
   Hughes North America rig count + international) + frac spread per
   well. Their thesis is the inverse of E&P's capex discipline: when
   E&P spends on capex, OFS revenue rises.
5. **Cracker / Chemicals downstream** — sitting on top of integrateds'
   petrochemical line. Read on ethane vs ethylene spreads, not on oil.

The senior analyst on any name in this pack must first locate the
name on the right axis, then read the *current* segment's pulse,
*then* derive the cross-segment read for the integrateds.

## Sources to lean on (priority order)

1. **EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report** — the inventory pulse, every
   Wednesday at 10:30 ET during the open. The leading indicator for
   crack spreads and WTI vs calendar. Read inventory builds/draws
   against the 5-year seasonal norm.
2. **EIA Drilling Productivity Report** — for tight oil, this IS the
   supply curve. Per-basin new-well production per rig by basin
   tells you what next-month production will be (approximately).
3. **Baker Hughes North America rig count** — Friday noon ET release.
   The capex pulse. When US oil rigs down for 3 weeks in a row the
   cycle is rolled over upstream.
4. **WTI vs Brent spread** — when >$5/bbl, the US has dropped export
   competitiveness and the integrateds are lagging international
   price realisations. Read when US export S&D matters.
5. **Companies' own "Finding and development cost" line in 10-K** —
   the *only* time the company's own segment table disagrees with the
   sell-side F&D estimate is when the company has admitted a
   structural cost change. Read it.
6. **OPEC+ communiqués** — directional guidance but not the price
   peg. Read for *intent* (who is deferring / cutting), not for the
   actual mb/d change announced.

## Bidirectional triggers

### Positive triggers

- WTI rises through the median F&D cost for the relevant basin ⇒
  supply-pulse from new wells re-accelerates *but* Economics of
  legacy inventory improves simultaneously. Net positive.
- EIA crude inventory draw > 5 MMbbl vs 5-yr seasonal norm + WTI moves
  +3% inside 30 days ⇒ refining margin holding up; positive for VLO
  / MPC / PSX.
- International rig count rises + NA rig count stable ⇒ OFS cycle
  globalising (positive SLB / HAL / BKR) — E&P activity not yet
  shifting to US-only.
- Integrated capex / cash-flow ratio < 60% AND buyback / dividend
  coverage > 1.5x ⇒ capex discipline holding (positive XOM /
  CVX); the integrated thesis doesn't get free-killed.
- OPEC+ extends voluntary cuts *and* a non-OPEC producer (Norway,
  Brazil, Guyana) absorbs the lift ⇒ supply-pulse managed; price
  likely to persist at the lifted level.

### Negative triggers

- WTI falls below bottom-decile F&D cost for two consecutive weeks
  + NA rig count down 3 weeks ⇒ capex is about to compress; OFS
  names pre-emptively.
- US WTI-Brent spreads >$5/bbl widening *and* US crude exports
  flat ⇒ US is running out of export capacity; integrateds will
  underprice their US barrels.
- Refinery utilisation < 85% + unplanned maintenance + gasoline
  inventory < 5-yr min ⇒ crack spread is going to compress badly;
  negative VLO / MPC / PSX.
- A large integrated announces a major acquisition in a non-core
  basin (e.g. XOM acquiring LNG terminal equity) ⇒ capital
  allocation thesis erodes.
- E&P F&D cost > mid-cycle WTI realisation sustainably ⇒ every
  dollar of capex destroys value. Sources of cash become dividend
  cuts. Read carefully — usually means the cycle is in late stage.

## Common biases to watch for

- **Brent-only blindness**: half the US supply-pulse is in WTI and
  Cushing vs Brent spread. Read both.
- **Sustained price = sustained capex fallacy**: when oil price is
  up 6 months, you do NOT assume the E&P is now sustainably high-
  capex. Read the 8-K on each budget cycle; capex discipline often
  holds *because* of capital allocation framework, not in spite
  thereof.
- **Inventory-levitation bias**: a single EIA build/draw does not
  equal a trend. Read the 4-week moving average, never the single
  print.
- **OPEC+ over-reliance**: OPEC+ announcements frequently do not
  behave in the oil price. The price response to a 1 mb/d cut is
  often less than the sellers expect.
- **F&D cost reporting bias**: companies under-report their full-
  cycle F&D by including only drilling + completion, not
  acquisition. Read the methodology note.
- **Refiner seasonal fallacy**: refining margins ALWAYS look bad
  before the maintenance season and ALWAYS recover. The turn is
  discretionary maintenance + 91 RVP slate shift.

| Refining margin | Realised crack spread | Realised crack spread normalised for maint schedule |

| Capex discipline | Capex / Revenues | Capex / pre-dividend FCF |

## What an attacker would say about this pack

- "You treat EIA WPSR as the leading indicator but it's actually a
  coincident indicator for the integrateds' quarterly results. Read
  inventory *expectations* (analyst consensus median) alongside the
  print to separate noise from signal."
- "OPEC+ communiqués give direction but the integrateds have long
  since stopped caring — they all sit on the long curve. The pack
  overweight-puts the OPEC+ signal in the positive-trigger gate,
  which can mislead when the consensus turns short."
- "Your pack ignores natural-gas exposure as a positive or negative
  vector. Most major integrateds run dual oil+gas books and the
  cross-cycle timing of those two is the real story 50% of the
  time."
- "The OFS rubric conflates SLB's international mix with HAL /
  BKR's US slant. A single positive trigger (NA rig count stable)
  is meaningful for HAL but small for SLB — they have a 70/30 vs
  30/70 split. Re-tier the trigger sensitivity."

## Pack failure modes

- The name is a pure-play renewables operator (e.g. solar / wind
  IPP) — out of scope; load a different pack.
- Operating in a region with a fixed domestic-realisation regime
  (e.g. Indonesia, Saudi Arabia) — capex discipline on the upstream
  side runs counter to the typical US-based read.
- Macro is in a deflationary environment *and* inventories are
  building → pack's vector-inversion path; consider a macro overlay
  from FRED before reading.
